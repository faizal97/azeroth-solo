# v10 story: The Black Ledger

Status: draft for review (2026-09-29). Once approved, it replaces the story in `docs/lore/canon.md`, and the cutscenes,
Lore Journal and quest text are rewritten to it.

## Why a new plot, not just new names

Today's story is Warcraft's, beat for beat: the king lost at sea, the stonemasons cheated of their pay, the marshal
who follows the trail to a mountain, and the court adviser who is really a black dragon. Renamed, it would still be
Blizzard's story. v10 keeps what the game is built on (a debt of blood and gold, a conspiracy in the capital, a
mountain of fire, a dragon in the marsh, a drowned isle), but the people, their reasons and the twist are ours.

**The idea in one line:** Caldreth was rebuilt after the Long War on borrowed gold, and the kingdom does not know who
it borrowed from.

## The world at the start

- **The Long War** between the Accord and the Krugar ended twelve years ago in an exhausted truce. Both sides rebuilt
  on credit.
- **The Black Ledger** is a guild of lenders, brokers and debt collectors. It lent to farmers after the war, to towns, to
  dwarf lords and to the crown itself. Everyone owes the Ledger something. Nobody has met its master.
- **Kingsmere.** Old King Rhodric Aldane died last winter. Lord Regent Edmund Carrow rules until Prince Tamlin comes of
  age. Carrow is honest and drowning in paperwork. The crown's Mistress of Coin, **Lady Meriel Thorne**, keeps the
  books balanced, and nobody else can read them.
- **Cinderpeak.** Under the mountain the Slagborn dwarves of Emperor Haldor Grimmark dig deeper every year, toward
  the fire of **Vulcarn, the King Below**, who sleeps in the mountain's heart.

## The twist, and what makes it ours

- **Meriel Thorne is not a monster in disguise.** She is a human, and a true believer: she thinks debt is the only
  honest bond, and that a kingdom which cannot pay should belong to whoever it owes.
- **The Ledger's master is Veshmira,** a black dragon of the Black Brood. Dragons hoard gold. Veshmira found a better
  use for it: she lends it. Every coin she lends comes back with land attached. She does not want to rule the Accord.
  She wants to own it.
- **The crown's debt comes due on the day Prince Tamlin is crowned.** That is why Thorne needs the kingdom poor,
  distracted and in debt when that day arrives.

**The raids** follow from it: the Magma Throne (Vulcarn, woken by the digging Grimmark's debt paid for) and
Veshmira's Lair (the creditor herself). **The Legend** Lyveus Cloveus keeps his story; the cabal that condemned him
becomes nobles deep in the Ledger's debt, and "the dark master" they served is the creditor nobody has met.

## Deliberately different from Warcraft

- No king lost at sea (Rhodric is dead and buried).
- No disguised dragon at court (Thorne is human and chose her side).
- No cheated stonemasons (Blackwell is a ruined shipwright working off a debt).
- No brother-and-sister dragon rivalry, and no fire lord and dragon fighting over the mountain (Vale runs a vault;
  Vulcarn sleeps until the digging wakes him).
- The villain's goal is ownership, not destruction.

## The dungeon intros

The 19 first-entry intros keep their shape (three or four short shots) and are rewritten with the new names and, where
the Ledger touches them, its thread: the Smugglers' Deep (Blackwell's fleet), Cinderpeak Depths (Grimmark's debt), the
Pyre Abbey (zealots who burn debtors' records and debtors alike), and so on. The rest are local stories and mostly
need only the names.
